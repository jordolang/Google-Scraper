# 📞 Website Pitch Script

A step-by-step walkthrough for contacting a local business and pitching them a
website. Open it any time inside the app with **Ctrl+G** — this is the same file
the TUI reads, so edit the wording here to make it your own.

> Numbers below match `PRICING_BREAKDOWN.md`: Launchpad **$499** → **$400**
> email-exclusive → **$350** with the attribution option. Professional starts at
> **$1,499+**. Enterprise is custom.

---

## 1. Before You Reach Out

Quick prep so you sound like you already know them:

- **Their name & business** — use it in the first sentence.
- **Do they already have a website?** No site → lead with "get found online."
  Dated site → lead with "modernize and speed it up."
- **Their rating / reviews** — a 4.5★+ reputation is a great compliment and a
  natural opener.
- **Their category** — tailor one concrete example ("electricians like you
  usually get booked from Google searches at night").
- Have your calendar and the pricing tiers open.

---

## 2. Opening (Introduce Yourself)

> "Hi, is this **[Owner / Business Name]**? My name's **Jordan** with
> **Jlang.dev** — I build websites for local businesses here in the area. Do you
> have a quick minute?"

If they're busy:

> "Totally understand — when's a better time to reach you for two minutes? I'll
> keep it short."

---

## 3. The Hook (Why It Matters)

Pick the line that fits what you found:

- **No website:**
  > "I came across **[Business]** and noticed you don't have a website yet. These
  > days most people check online before they call — a simple, professional site
  > can turn those searches into booked jobs."

- **Outdated / slow website:**
  > "I found your current site — it's a good start, but I can make it load faster,
  > look modern on phones, and rank better on Google so more customers actually
  > find you."

- **Great reviews:**
  > "You've got a fantastic **[4.8★]** reputation — a website that matches that
  > quality would help you win the customers who are comparing you to bigger
  > competitors."

---

## 4. Discovery Questions

Let them talk — you'll tailor the pitch to their answers:

1. "How are most of your customers finding you right now?"
2. "Do you have a way for people to reach you or book online after hours?"
3. "Have you ever lost a job because someone couldn't find you online?"
4. "If you had a site, what would you most want it to do — calls, bookings,
   showing off your work?"

---

## 5. The Pitch (What You Offer)

> "Here's what I do: I build you a clean, mobile-friendly website — usually a
> **Launchpad** site is live within about a day of getting your content. It's
> built to show up on Google, load fast, and make it dead simple for customers
> to call or book you. You focus on the work; I handle the tech."

Tie it back to their answer:

- Wants more calls → "We put a click-to-call button front and center."
- Wants bookings → "We add an online booking / contact form so leads come in 24/7."
- Proud of their work → "We build a photo gallery that shows off your best jobs."

---

## 6. Pricing & the Email-Exclusive Offer

Lead with value, then the exclusive discount:

> "The **Launchpad Package** is normally **$499** — but because I reached out to
> you directly, you qualify for my **email-exclusive rate of $400**, which is
> **$99 off**. That's a complete, professional site, live fast."

If they hesitate on price, offer the attribution option:

> "There's also an optional **Attribution Option** that brings it to **$350** — a
> total of **$149 off**. All that means is a small, tasteful 'Developed by
> Jlang.dev' credit in your footer. It doesn't touch your branding or content,
> and you can remove it later anytime."

If they need more than a starter site:

> "If you want something bigger — custom design, e-commerce, lots of pages — the
> **Professional Package** starts at **$1,499+**, and we do fully custom
> **Enterprise** builds too. We can start with Launchpad and grow from there."

---

## 7. Handling Common Objections

**"I don't have the time / I'm too busy."**
> "That's exactly why I handle everything — I just need a few details and photos,
> and I do the rest. Launchpad sites are usually live within a day of that."

**"I already have a website."**
> "Nice — how's it working for you? A lot of folks I talk to have a site that's
> slow or hard to use on a phone. I'm happy to take a quick look and tell you
> honestly if it's worth updating."

**"It's too expensive / money's tight."**
> "I hear you. That's why the email rate is **$400**, and the attribution option
> drops it to **$350**. One or two extra jobs from the site usually pays for it."

**"I get all my business from word of mouth / Facebook."**
> "That's great, and a website works right alongside that — it's where people go
> to check you out before they call, and you own it instead of renting space on
> someone else's platform."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Absolutely. Can I email you a quick summary and a couple examples of sites
> I've built? What's the best address? Then I'll follow up in a few days."

---

## 8. Closing / Next Steps

Ask for a clear yes-or-next-step:

> "Want me to get started on the Launchpad site at the $400 rate? I'll send over a
> short list of what I need — your logo, a few photos, and your hours — and we'll
> have you online fast."

If not ready:

> "No problem. I'll send you the details and a couple examples, and check back in
> a few days. Sound fair?"

Always confirm the next action before you hang up: an email address, a follow-up
date, or a start.

---

## 9. Voicemail & Gatekeeper

**Voicemail:**
> "Hi **[Name]**, this is Jordan with Jlang.dev — I build websites for local
> businesses and had a quick idea for **[Business]**. Give me a call back at
> **[number]**, or I'll follow up by email. Thanks!"

**Gatekeeper (receptionist / staff):**
> "Hi! I help local businesses with their websites — is the owner or whoever
> handles the website around for a quick minute? If not, no worries, what's the
> best way to reach them?"

---

_You've got this. Be friendly, listen more than you talk, and always end with a
clear next step._
